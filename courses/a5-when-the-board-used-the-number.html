When the board already used the number

Some incidents are found after the damage is a decision, not a dashboard. Quarrybank's board pack carried a margin figure built on the bad load, and by the time finance noticed, prices had already been held in two regions. Tonight is about the part of incident response that happens in the audit committee: which decisions used the wrong figure, whether to restate, footnote or correct next period, and who gets to call it material.

Part 0

When fixing the table is the easy part

Session 1 named three kinds of data incident. The third, the decision incident, is the one security frameworks rarely name, because in security the harm is usually the event itself. In a decision incident the data was only wrong for a while, and the harm is what somebody did while it was wrong. The warehouse can be corrected in an afternoon. The price that was held, the certificate that was signed and the update that was sent cannot be corrected by anyone on the data team.

★ What you walk out with tonight. A way to trace a wrong figure into every decision that used it, three correction routes and when each one applies, an honest view of why materiality is argued rather than looked up, and page 5 of Quarrybank's incident response plan: a restatement rule that says when the board pack is restated, when it is footnoted, who decides, who signs, and who is told by when.

LiveThe incident the finance controller dreads3 min▶

A quality incident has a clean finish line: every consumer reads corrected rows. A decision incident does not. Once a number has been read in a board meeting, it lives in minutes, in emails, in a pricing committee's slide and in a certificate a lender holds. Correcting the source table changes none of those. The response has to follow the number out of the warehouse and into every room it reached.

That makes the decision incident a leadership problem more than a technical one. The on-call engineer can fix the margin calculation. Only the finance controller, the chief financial officer and, at Quarrybank, the audit committee chair can decide what the organisation says about the figures it already used.

Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example. The monthly board pack went out on 2026-03-03 at 08:00 UTC. Its margin figure was overstated because it used the bad load from February, the duplicated supplier partition that doubled revenue for two regions. Nobody could see that from the pack. A finance analyst's reconciliation flagged it 48 hours later. The first outside complaint, from the external auditor, came at 168 hours.

Part 1 · covers tracing which decisions used the wrong figure

Follow the number into the room

5 min live

The first job in a decision incident is a list: every place the wrong figure was read, and every decision that was taken on it, with a time against each. The times matter more than in any other incident type, because they split the list in two. Decisions taken before the first signal could not have been stopped by any response plan. Decisions taken after it are the cost of declaring late.

LiveWhy fixing the table does not fix the decision3 min▶

A corrected table changes what the next reader sees. It does not reach back into the meeting where prices were held, the email that carried the investor update, or the certificate a lender filed. Each of those is now a separate artefact with its own owner, outside the warehouse and outside the data team's reach.

So the trace for a decision incident has two columns the quality trace does not: what was decided on it, and who owns undoing or confirming that decision. For Quarrybank the pricing committee owns the held prices, investor relations owns the update, the chief financial officer owns the covenant certificate and finance owns the bonus accrual. The data team owns none of them, and has to tell all of them.

Ask "what was decided on it?", not only "who read it?" A consumer trace counts readers. A decision trace counts acts. The second list is shorter and every item on it is more expensive.

LiveThe decision no plan can reach2 min▶

The pricing committee held prices in two regions 24 hours after the pack went out. The finance reconciliation flagged the margin 24 hours after that. No response plan starts before its first signal, so no response plan could have stopped that decision. The best a plan can do is tell the pricing committee quickly, so the decision can be revisited.

Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example, measured on the bench. With no plan, the board deck incident is declared 120h00 after the signal, when the auditor complains, and the wrong margin reaches 5 consumers and 4 decisions, with 35 bad reads by 17 people. With a declare rule (R1) it is declared 0h15 after the signal and the count falls to 3 consumers and 1 decision. That 1 decision, "Hold prices in two regions", stays on every rung of the bench.

What happens before the first signal is a detection problem: how long it took a reconciliation to notice the margin had moved. That belongs to the observability course. Tonight takes the signal as given.

Part 2 · covers restate vs footnote vs correct next period, and IAS 8

Restate, footnote, or fix it next time

5 min live

There are three honest ways to correct a figure the board has already seen. Restate it: reissue the corrected figure and say plainly that the earlier one was wrong. Footnote it: carry a correction note in the next pack and tell the people who acted on it. Correct it next period: fix the source and let the next pack be right. Which one fits depends less on the size of the error than on where the figure went and what was done with it.

LiveWhat IAS 8 says, and why it is only an analogy here3 min▶

A finance controller asked "do we restate?" reaches for IAS 8, the international standard that covers errors in financial statements. Its rule is short: "an entity corrects material prior period errors retrospectively by restating the comparative amounts", with an escape for when it cannot be done: "Unless it is impracticable to determine the effects of the error". The standard was retitled in April 2024 to "Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements".

A board pack is management information, not published financial statements, so IAS 8 does not bind it. It is the analogy, not the rule. What it lends the board pack is a shape worth copying: a material error is corrected by putting the earlier figure right in the record, not only by getting the next one right, and "impracticable" is a narrow escape that has to be argued, not assumed.

Say "by analogy with IAS 8" in the room, out loud. It stops two arguments at once: the one that says the standard forces a restatement of a management pack, and the one that says the standard is irrelevant because the pack is internal.

RouteWhat happensWhen it fitsWhat it costs
RestateThe corrected figure is reissued and the error is namedThe figure left the building: an investor, a lender, an auditor relied on itAttention, and the questions every restatement invites

The routes are not a ranking of severity. A small error that reached a lender may need a restatement; a larger one that nobody acted on may not. Quarrybank's own rung on the bench decides its route: with no plan the investor update and the covenant certificate carried the wrong margin out of the building; with a declare rule only the price decision did.

Part 3 · covers materiality as the disagreement it is

Materiality is argued, not looked up

5 min live

Every correction route above turns on one word: material. Was the error big enough, or important enough, that the people who used the figure would have acted differently? Two serious views answer that differently, and a leader's job is to know both and decide who settles it, not to pretend there is a table to look it up in.

LiveTwo views of material, taught as a disagreement3 min▶

The quantitative view sets a threshold: an error is material if it moves the figure by more than an agreed amount. Its strength is consistency. The same error is treated the same way every month, and nobody has to win an argument at 9pm. Its weakness is that a threshold measures the number, not what was done with it.

The qualitative view asks what the error did. Did it change a decision? Did it reach a lender or an investor? Did it cross a line that matters, such as a covenant? Its strength is that it follows the harm. Its weakness is that it needs judgement, and judgement under pressure tends to find reasons why this one is not material.

Practitioners hold both, and they disagree about which leads. There is no threshold number here, because none was read at source and any number quoted would be a guess presented as a rule. The IAS 8 materiality definition was clarified in 2018; its text was not read, so it is not paraphrased here.

Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example. Quarrybank's controller argues the margin error is not material on its own size. The audit committee chair argues it is material because it informed a price decision and, with no plan, reached a lender's covenant certificate. Both are right about what they are measuring. The restatement rule exists so that this argument has an owner and a deadline instead of a winner.

Self-studyHow a regulator ties a clock to a materiality call3 min▶

The clearest public example of a clock attached to a materiality determination is SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05. It is due "four business days after a registrant determines that a cybersecurity incident is material", and describes the "material aspects of the incident's nature, scope, and timing". It applies to US registrants and to cybersecurity incidents only. Quarrybank is not a registrant, and a margin error is not a cybersecurity incident.

The shape is still worth borrowing. The clock starts at the determination, not at the event, so the speed of the determination becomes the thing to manage. Quarrybank's constructed policy does the same for its own board: the audit committee chair is told within 48 hours of anyone becoming aware of a misstated board figure.

Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example, measured on the bench. Becoming aware is the 48h00 reconciliation, so the chair notice is due 96h00 after the bad write. With only a declare rule (R1) the chair is told at 168h15 after the bad write: missed, because nobody owns the notice. With severity and roles (R2) the chair is told at 72h35: met. A declare rule starts the clock; it takes a named owner to beat it.

Trace the margin, then write the restatement rule

★ 22 min · everyone builds

No code. Open the board deck incident on three rungs of the bench, list every consumer and decision the wrong margin reached, write the correction notice Quarrybank sends to its board, and then write page 5 of the incident response plan so the next one is routed before anyone argues.

Open the board deck on R0, no plan. In the bench, open "Wrong number on the board deck: what it reached". Write down every consumer and every decision, with its time after the bad write. Mark each decision as before or after the 48h00 signal.

★ Step 1 · decision trace, Quarrybank board deck, R0consumers reached board portal copy; emailed pack (9 directors); pricing committee deck; investor update; lender covenant certificate decisions on it Hold prices in two regions 24h00 BEFORE signal Investor update sent with margin 72h00 after Covenant certificate signed 100h00 after Bonus accrual set from the pack 133h20 after declared 120h00 after the signal (the auditor's complaint)

Switch to R1, declare rule. Declared 0h15 after the signal. The investor update, the covenant certificate and the bonus accrual never happen on the bad figure, and the list falls to 3 consumers and 1 decision. Note what is left: the emailed pack and the pricing deck holders are still never told, and the chair deadline is still missed.

Switch to R4, lineage-driven notice. Same 3 consumers, same 1 decision. Never told for this incident goes from 2 under R1 to 0: the emailed pack and the pricing deck holders are told because lineage says they read the pack. Now look at "Hold prices in two regions". It is still there. R4 tells the pricing committee; it cannot undo the price decision. That decision is what the correction notice is for.

Write Quarrybank's correction notice for the board. One page, plain words, no defence. It names the figure, what was wrong with it, which decision used it, and what happens next. Do not put a corrected margin value in it until finance has one; say the figure was overstated and why.

★ Step 4 · correction notice, Quarrybank boardTo: board directors; audit committee chair; pricing committee From: chief financial officer Subject: Correction to the margin figure in the 3 March board pack The margin figure in the board pack of 3 March was overstated. It was calculated on sales data that contained a duplicated supplier load from February, which doubled reported revenue for two regions. Decisions that used it: the pricing committee's decision to hold prices in two regions. Please treat that decision as open for review. What happens next: a corrected figure, signed by finance, by [date]. Route under our restatement rule: [restate / footnote], decided by [name], because [the figure left the building / a decision used it]. Questions: [finance controller, contact].

Write the restatement rule. Page 5 of Quarrybank's incident response plan. Its job is to route the next wrong board figure before anyone argues about size, and to make sure the materiality call has an owner and a deadline.

★ Step 5 · plan, leader page 5, restatement ruleQUARRYBANK INCIDENT RESPONSE PLAN · leader page 5 · restatement rule Applies to: any figure in a board pack, investor update or lender document found to be wrong after it was sent. WHEN WE RESTATE the figure left the building (investor, lender, auditor, anyone outside), OR the materiality owner calls it material WHEN WE FOOTNOTE the figure stayed inside AND a decision was taken on it: note in the next pack, every decider told in writing now NEXT PERIOD ONLY nobody acted on it AND nobody outside saw it: fix, log WHO DECIDES finance controller proposes; CFO decides materiality, by analogy with IAS 8, quantitative AND qualitative WHO SIGNS CFO signs every restatement and every correction notice WHO IS TOLD every consumer and every decider on the trace, by name BY WHEN audit committee chair: within 48 hours of becoming aware (policy input); route decided before the chair is told A decision taken before the first signal is listed, never hidden.

Route first, argue size second. Where the figure went and what was done with it can be checked in an hour. How material it was can take a week of meetings. Let the checkable question choose the route, and give the argument a named owner and a clock.

Homework

Before session 6

30 min
  1. Take your last board pack, or the nearest equivalent. For its three most-used figures, list every place each one went after the meeting: decks, emails, certificates, filings.
  2. Find out who in your organisation decides whether an error in management information is material. If the answer is "it depends", write down who it depended on last time.
  3. Read your restatement rule to your finance controller. Change every route they would argue with, and note the arguments you kept.
  4. Check whether any policy, contract or lender agreement sets a clock for telling someone about a wrong figure. Write each one on the rule as an input, with its owner.
Check yourself

Three questions

Quarrybank has corrected the margin calculation in the warehouse. Why is the decision incident not over?

A corrected table changes what the next reader sees. It cannot reach back into decisions already taken on the old figure, so the response has to follow the number to every person who acted on it.

On the bench, which board deck decision survives every plan from R1 to R5, and why?

No response plan starts before its first signal. The bonus accrual and the covenant certificate happen after the signal and disappear once the incident is declared 0h15 after it.

Quarrybank's controller says the error is not material by size; the chair says it is, because it informed a price decision. How should the restatement rule treat this?

Quantitative and qualitative views of materiality both have merit and practitioners disagree on which leads. No threshold was read at source, and a board pack can still reach a lender. The rule gives the argument an owner and a clock.
