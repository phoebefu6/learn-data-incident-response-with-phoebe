Part 0

The trace is the list of everyone you will eventually tell

A consumer trace sounds like an engineering chore. It is really the first draft of three later jobs: what you stop in session 3, what you correct and check in session 5, and who you notify in session 6. Every name missing from the trace tonight is a person who will hear about the problem from someone other than you, or not at all.

Live = presented in the 45 minutes Self-study = the full depth, after class 🔍 Every diagram zooms

★ What you walk out with tonight. A consumer trace for Quarrybank's bad load, with every copy sorted into "refreshes at correction" or "must be told", the decisions written down next to the copies, and page 2 of the responder half of the plan: a template table you can fill in under pressure.

LiveWhy the list matters more than the graph3 min▶

Lineage tools draw beautiful graphs. A graph is not what you need at 03:00. You need a list: each thing that read the bad rows, when it read them, whether it will clean itself up when the source is fixed, and who owns it. The graph is one input to that list, and as Part 3 shows, not the only one.

The SRE book asks the incident commander to keep a living incident document. The consumer trace lives inside it, under "consumers reached so far", and it grows while the incident runs. Write each consumer down the moment you find it, with the proof of how you found it, rather than saving the list for the end.

Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example. The bad load was found by a volume monitor at 02:40Z. By then the revenue dashboard, the finance mart, the weekly partner extract and the forecast model retrain had all read the duplicated partition. Nobody on call knew the partner extract had already left, because nobody had asked what ran after the load.

Self-studySix kinds of consumer2 min▶
  • Tables built from the bad table: marts, aggregates, snapshots.
  • Dashboards that query those tables, live or on a schedule.
  • Extracts sent out of the warehouse: files to partners, feeds to other systems.
  • Scheduled emails that render a number into a message and send it to a list.
  • Models trained or scored on the bad rows, which carry the error in their weights or outputs.
  • Decisions people made after reading any of the above. These are the hardest to find and the most expensive to miss.
Part 1 · covers the consumer trace since the bad write

Every copy since 02:00Z, sorted by how it clears

6 min live

Start the trace at the bad write, not at the signal. Everything that read the partition after 02:00Z is a candidate, including the things that read it in the forty minutes before any alarm. Then sort each one by the only question that changes your next move: when the source is corrected, does this copy correct itself, or does it stay wrong until a person is told?

LiveRefreshes at correction, or must be told3 min▶

A dashboard that queries the mart shows the right number the next time it refreshes after the fix. A file already delivered to the partner does not. A model already retrained on doubled revenue does not. An email already sitting in 40 inboxes certainly does not. Those copies left the reach of your warehouse when they were made, and the only way to clear them is to reach their owner.

Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example. The lineage query returned the mart and both dashboards straight away. The partner extract and the model retrain showed up only when the on-call engineer asked the orchestrator what had run after 02:00Z. Two query results, one list, two proof links.

Part 2 · covers exposure as a number that grows every hour

Exposure grows for as long as the door is open

5 min live

The bad load's list grows with every scheduled job that fires before you stop it. The leaked table shows the same thing as a count: the longer it stays reachable, the more rows somebody fetches. NIST is honest about how hard this is: "Determining the incident's magnitude is often one of the most challenging aspects" of the work. It is also the number everyone asks for first.

LiveWhy 18,400 and 7,600 are the same incident3 min▶

The leaked table is identical on every rung of the bench: 18,400 customer rows, shared through a public link and a vendor workspace share at 09:00Z on 24 February. An unknown address began paging the public link 3h10 after the share, a few hundred rows every few minutes, and kept going until the link was closed. So the number of rows exposed is not a property of the leak. It is a property of the response.

Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example, measured on the bench. Even the best plan leaves 7,600 rows exposed, because the paging began before the access-log alert fired at 3h20. What happens before the first signal is detection, which the observability course owns. What happens after it is yours, and on this table it is the difference between 7,600 and every row.

Self-studyReport the number with its time2 min▶

Because exposure moves, a bare number in a status update is wrong by the next update. Write it as "rows exposed as of 12:40Z: N, still open" or "rows exposed, final, door closed 12:50Z: N", with the access-log query as proof. The privacy partner needs the final figure and the time it became final; they own what it means under the GDPR's 72 hours and the PDPA's 3 calendar days, taught in the GDPR ↗ and PDPA ↗ breach sessions. Closing the doors themselves is session 4.

Part 3 · covers the trace you cannot get from lineage

Decisions and inboxes never appear in the graph

5 min live

Warehouse lineage stops at the warehouse wall. It can tell you a job exported a file; it cannot tell you who opened it. It can tell you a dashboard queried the mart; it cannot tell you that a regional manager read it at 07:00 and placed an order. The most consequential consumers of bad data are people, and you find them by asking, not by querying.

LiveThree decisions already made on the bad load3 min▶

With no plan, the bad load reaches three decisions before anyone stops it: a replenishment order for the two regions whose revenue doubled, a promotion budget cut in the other regions that suddenly looked weak by comparison, and the partner's volume rebate calculation, which ran on the extract. None of those is a table. Each has an owner who acted in good faith on a wrong number and does not yet know it.

Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example, measured on the bench. With no plan the bad load reaches 5 consumers and 3 decisions, and none of the 5 is ever told. The partner's rebate is the one with a contract attached: the partner contract notice is missed on that rung.

LiveHow to find what lineage cannot2 min▶
  • Ask the owner of every copy who uses it. The sales email owner knows the 40 recipients. The partner manager knows what the partner does with the extract.
  • Read the dashboard access log for the window. Who opened the revenue dashboard between 02:15Z and the fix is a list of people who might have acted.
  • Ask the leader running the incident to ask upward. "Was any order, budget or price set on this number since 02:00Z?" is a question a responder cannot answer and a head of data can.
  • Log exports going forward. An export that writes its destination and recipients into the catalogue turns tomorrow's outside copy into an inside one.
Self-studyKeep the list inside the living document2 min▶

The SRE book's "living incident document" is where the trace belongs, not in a spreadsheet on someone's laptop. As consumers are found, they are added; as they are notified or confirmed corrected, their row gains a time and a proof. Session 5 uses the same rows to check every copy reads corrected data, and session 6 sends the notices from them.

Build-along

Trace the bad load on the bench

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Open the bench on R0, no plan, and read what the bad load reached. Then switch to R2, severity and roles, and watch the list shrink, but not to zero. The two survivors are the lesson: copies made in the first 35 minutes after the bad write stay on the list whatever you do next.

Open "Bad load: what it reached" on R0. The bench starts on no plan. Expand the bad load's panel and copy the list into your record: 5 consumers (executive dashboard, sales email, partner extract, forecast model, finance mart), 3 decisions, 60 bad reads by 52 people. Under R0 none of the 5 is told.

Sort the five. For each, write the copy time after the bad write from Part 1, and mark it "refreshes at correction" or "must be told". The executive dashboard and finance mart refresh; the extract, the model and the email do not.

Switch to R2 and read the panel again. Severity and roles bring containment to 2h35 after the signal, so the bench now shows 2 consumers, the partner extract and the forecast model, and 2 decisions. The executive dashboard and the sales email were scheduled for 4h00 and 5h00 after the bad write; containment at 3h15 after the bad write stopped them happening at all.

Explain why two stay. The extract left at 0h30 and the model retrained at 0h35, both before the 0h40 signal. No response plan can un-send them; only telling their owners clears them. Note that R3, the containment playbook, gives the same list: faster containment cannot reach back before the signal. The bench models times by stated rules, so these shifts come from the plan, not from luck.

Save the template. Keep the shape, drop Quarrybank's rows. This is page 2 of the responder half of the plan.

A consumer you cannot name an owner for is still a row. Write "owner unknown" and keep it on the list. An empty owner cell is a task; a missing row is a person who never hears from you.

Homework

Before session 3

30 min
  1. Pick one important table where you work and list every consumer downstream of it, using the six kinds from Part 0. Mark each "refreshes at correction" or "must be told".
  2. Find every export, extract and scheduled email that leaves your warehouse. For each, check whether its destination and recipients are logged anywhere a responder could query at 3am.
  3. Name one decision in your business that is made directly from a dashboard. Write down who makes it and how you would reach them during an incident.
  4. Fill in the consumer trace template for the last real data problem you handled, and note every row you could only add by asking a person.
Check yourself

Three questions

The source partition has been corrected and re-run. Which of the bad load's copies are still wrong?

Dashboards and tables built on the source refresh at correction. Copies that left the warehouse, a delivered file, a retrained model, an email in 40 inboxes, stay wrong until their owner hears from you.

On the bench the leaked table exposes 18,400 rows under R1 and 7,600 under R5. Why the difference?

Same table, same leak, same signal time. Exposure is a function of containment time, which is why it has to be reported with an "as of" time until the door is closed.

Why does the consumer trace need more than the lineage query?

Warehouse lineage stops at the warehouse wall. The replenishment order, the promotion cut and the partner's rebate were made by people on the bad number and appear in no graph.
