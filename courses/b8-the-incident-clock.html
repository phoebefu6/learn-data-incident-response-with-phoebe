Part 0

What the clock measures

The clock is an event-driven replay, not an animation. Quarrybank's quarter is stored as a log of 222 timestamped events: every time a downstream copy was made from bad data, every read of a dashboard or email, every access through an open door, every decision taken on a number. A plan fixes when each incident is declared, assessed, contained and corrected, and who is told. The engine walks the log against those times and counts what got through.

The headline that ends the "we will improvise" argument: 14 consumers and 7 decisions on bad data with no plan, 5 and 1 with a rehearsed one, and every notice deadline met. The floor you cannot go below with any response plan. And Quarrybank's incident response plan, both halves, scored.

LiveThe rules the engine applies4 min▶
  • Tainted: a consumer that made its copy from bad data before containment landed. Containment stops new copies; it does not fix the ones already made.
  • Cleared: a tainted copy stops counting when it is corrected or when its users are told. Dashboards and tables refresh at the correction. Copies that left the warehouse, the sales email, the partner extract, the forecast model, the board pack, only clear when someone tells their users.
  • Doors: on the leaked table, an access counts while its door is open. A containment playbook closes every door; without one, only the public link closes at containment and the vendor share stays open until the close-out.
  • Plan times, all in minutes after the bad write: declare 15 after the first signal with a rule, 5 when rehearsed, at the first complaint without one; containment 15 after assessment with a playbook, 120 without; correction 360 after containment, 180 rehearsed.
  • Deadlines are fixed inputs, not taught here: GDPR 72 hours from awareness and PDPA 3 calendar days after assessment for the leak, owned by the GDPR ↗ and PDPA ↗ courses; a constructed 24-hour partner contract term for the bad load and a constructed 48-hour audit-committee policy for the board deck.
Self-studyWhat is real here3 min▶
  • Computed: every count on the bench, live in your browser from the event log. A Python reference implementation produces the same numbers on every plan, every trap, the trap switches on the rehearsed plan and two sets of hand-typed move times: 1,151 values compared, none different.
  • Constructed: Quarrybank, its consumers, readers, row ranges, event times, the partner contract term and the audit-committee policy. No public incident dataset was used, so no real-world frequency is claimed.
  • Modelled crudely, on purpose: every bad read counts the same; a regional manager glancing at a dashboard weighs as much as a director quoting a margin to a lender. A real plan would weight them, and the first extension of the bench would too.

LiveReading the flat rungs3 min▶

Two rungs leave the headline where it was, and both are worth more than they look.

  • R3, the containment playbook, keeps 7 + 3 because the consumers it would have protected were already reached before R2's slower containment landed. What it changes is in the details: customer rows exposed on the leaked table fall from 12,800 to 8,400, and containment arrives 0h50 after the signal instead of 2h35.
  • R5, rehearsal, keeps 5 + 1 because R4 had already reached the floor. What it buys is time: declare, contain and correct at 0h05, 0h25 and 3h25 after the signal instead of 0h15, 0h50 and 6h50, and 7,600 rows exposed instead of 8,400. Those three times are the readiness numbers leader session 6 asks you to report.

Part 2 · covers the three traps and the floor

Three good ideas at 3am, and the floor under all of them

6 min live

A bench that can only get better measures nothing. Each trap below is one plausible move added to a strong plan, and each one reports a worse number than the plan without it.

TrapCompared withWhat gets worse
T1 Fix before you containR47 + 1 instead of 5 + 1; people who read bad data 37 instead of 13; leaked rows 18,400 instead of 8,400
T2 Drop the bad table to stop itR4Leaked rows 18,400 assumed instead of 8,400 measured; correction 10h50 instead of 6h50; evidence lost
T3 Tell only the people who complainedR57 + 3 instead of 5 + 1; 4 consumers never told; 4 copies still wrong; partner deadline missed
LiveWhy each trap is tempting, and what it costs4 min▶
  • Fix before you contain feels efficient: why stop the load if the fix is an hour away? Because the fix took 6h50, and every copy made in the meantime is another consumer. On the bench the sales email to 40 people goes out inside that window. NIST's word for the alternative is plain: "Containment refers to preventing the expansion of an incident."
  • Drop the bad table feels decisive. It also deletes the access history, so the leaked table's exposure can no longer be measured and every row has to be assumed exposed. Responder session 1 has the full argument.
  • Tell only the people who complained feels proportionate. The people who complained are the ones who noticed; the forecast model and the partner extract did not complain, and they are the copies that stay wrong.
Self-studyThe floor belongs to detection2 min▶

Five consumers and one decision survive the best response plan, because they were reached before the first signal existed. No declare rule can fire before a signal. Moving the floor means a faster signal: a monitor on the share settings that fires when a link becomes public, a reconciliation that runs before the board pack is sent rather than two days after. That is the observability course's work (lineage and blast radius ↗), and it is the right request to make in the postmortem.

Build-along

Run the quarter, then score the plan

★ 25 min · everyone builds

The full bench is below: every plan, every trap, the seven plan pages as switches, and your own move times per incident. Work the steps in order and write each number down; the last step turns them into the plan's scorecard.

Start at R0 and climb to R5. Click each rung in turn. Copy the headline, never told and deadlines met for each into your notes. Check them against the table in Part 1; if any differs, reload the page, because the bench and the table come from the same engine.

Open the three "what it reached" panels on R0 and on R5. On R0 the board deck reaches five consumers, including the investor update and the lender's covenant certificate, both outside the building. On R5 it reaches three, all inside, and one decision that predates the signal.

Spring the traps. Click T1, T2 and T3 and compare each with the rung it modifies, as in the Part 2 table. Then click R5 and tick each trap switch on its own. Fix before you contain lifts the leaked rows from 7,600 to 14,800; dropping the table lifts them to 18,400 assumed and pushes every correction from 3h25 to 7h25. The headline stays at the floor both times, which is why the second counters are on the bench.

Type your own moves. Click R0, then for each incident type these minutes after the bad write and press replay: bad load 60, 90, 300; leaked table 240, 250, 600; board deck 2900, 2910, 3000. These are fast, confident times with no plan pages behind them. The bench reports 7 consumers and 3 decisions, 4 never told and 0 of 4 deadlines met. Speed without roles, lineage and a notice owner meets no deadline at all.

Score your own quarter the same way. The bench's rules fit on half a page. Take your last three incidents, list every copy and decision with a timestamp, and replay them against the times your plan would have produced. The first time a team does this, the never-told count is the number that changes the conversation.

Check yourself

Three questions

Which single plan page cuts Quarrybank's never-told count from 4 to 0?

R3 to R4 adds the lineage notice: never told goes from 4 to 0, copies still wrong from 4 to 0, and the partner deadline is met. Containment stops new copies; it does not tell anyone about the old ones.

On the rehearsed plan, 5 consumers and 1 decision still used bad data. Why can no response plan remove them?

The vendor's download, the unknown address's first pages, the board pack reads and the pricing decision all come before their incident's first signal. A declare rule cannot fire before a signal.

Your team types fast move times on the bench but has no roles, lineage notice or notice owner. What does the bench report?

Fast containment helps the headline, but without lineage nobody tells the copies outside the warehouse, and without roles nobody owns the formal notices.
