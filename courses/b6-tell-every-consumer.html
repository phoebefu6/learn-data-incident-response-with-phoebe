LiveWhy complaints are the wrong source3 min▶

A complaint tells you who could see the problem. A dashboard with revenue doubled for two regions is visible; someone frowns and messages the data team. A file already sitting on a partner's server, or a model that quietly retrained overnight, shows nothing wrong to the person using it. Their copies look normal, so they never complain, so a list built from complaints never includes them.

NIST asks that response procedures "include what must be reported to whom and at what times". The "whom" is not a judgement you make at 03:00 from memory. It is the output of the trace.

Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example. The bad load's first complaint arrived 7h10 after the bad write, from someone reading a dashboard. By then the partner extract had left at 0h30 and the forecast model had retrained at 0h35. Neither owner had anything to complain about: both copies looked exactly like a normal week.

Part 1 · covers the notice list generated from the trace

Complaints find the loud copies, lineage finds all of them

6 min live

The bench models two ways to build the list, by stated rules. With lineage-driven notice, every consumer the trace reached is told 30 minutes after containment, 15 when the plan is rehearsed. Without it, only the people who complained are told, 60 minutes after the incident is declared. The difference is not speed. It is who is on the list at all.

LiveTurn every trace row into a notice row3 min▶

Every row on the trace marked "must be told" in session 2 becomes one row on the notice list. Add three columns and nothing else.

Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example. The wrong margin figure on the board deck was signalled by the finance reconciliation 48h00 after it was written, and the first complaint came from the external auditor at 168h00. Nobody who received the emailed pack complained, and nobody who built the pricing deck from it did either. On every plan from R1 to R5 the bench shows those two and the portal as the consumers reached; only a list built from the trace names the emailed pack and the pricing deck.

Ask upward as well as down the lineage. A pricing deck is not in any warehouse lineage graph. It shows up because someone asked "was anything built or decided on this figure?" and wrote the answer on the trace.

Part 2 · covers what each kind of consumer needs from the notice

Four kinds of reader, four different asks

5 min live

"The data was wrong, sorry" is not a notice. Each consumer holds a different kind of copy, and each kind has one thing its owner must do to stop the wrong figure being used again. Lead with that action, then give the facts.

LiveExtracts and scheduled emails3 min▶
  • Extract recipients hold a file you cannot reach. Tell them to stop using it, name the file exactly (file name and the run that produced it), and say when a replacement will arrive. If they loaded it into their own systems, ask them to say so; that is a copy your trace cannot see.
  • Scheduled email recipients hold a number in their inbox. Reply on the original thread with the wrong figure, the corrected figure, and the period it covers. A fresh email with only the right number leaves the wrong one sitting above it.

Lead with the action. The first line of every notice is what the reader must do. The explanation is for the people who read on.

LiveReports and models3 min▶
  • Report reruns: a dashboard on the warehouse refreshes when the correction lands (session 5 proved that). An exported report, a slide or a spreadsheet does not. Its owner reruns it and confirms, and the confirmation goes in the acknowledged column.
  • Model retrain flags: a model trained on bad rows keeps producing slightly wrong output after the source is fixed. The notice asks the owner to flag it for retrain and to check anything it already fed. Rebuilding the partition it trained on is backfill mechanics ↗.
Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example. The forecast model retrained on the bad rows 0h35 after the bad write, five minutes before the volume monitor fired. Its forecast fed a replenishment order for the two affected regions. The model's notice asks for two things: retrain it, and have the buyer check that order before it ships.

Self-studyWhat a consumer notice leaves out2 min▶

Practice, not a finding: a consumer notice states what was wrong and what to do. It does not speculate about cause, assign blame, or promise a root-cause date. Those belong in the incident record and later in the write-up, which is the craft of the planned runbooks and postmortems course on the Learn with Phoebe hub ↗. If the notice concerns personal data, the privacy partner reads it before it goes, because a consumer notice can become a disclosure.

Part 3 · covers contract and legal deadlines as fixed inputs

Deadlines arrive as inputs, not as arguments

5 min live

Some notices have a clock attached. You do not set those clocks and you do not debate them at 03:00. Contract deadlines come from the contract owner and legal deadlines come from the privacy partner; your job is to know which consumers carry one, and to get their notice out well inside it.

The law is not taught on this page. The responder's part is mechanical and essential: put each deadline on the notice list as a fixed input next to the consumer it applies to, and make sure the record can show the sent time.

LiveThe partner clause, measured on the bench3 min▶
Real world

Constructed Quarrybank example, measured on the bench. Quarrybank's constructed partner contract says the partner must be told within 24 hours of Quarrybank becoming aware of an error in a delivered extract. Measured from the bad write, that is due at 24h40. Under R4 the partner is told at 2h00 and under R5 at 1h20, both met. Under R3 and T3, which tell only the complainers, the partner is never told and the deadline is missed. Twenty-four hours is plenty of time; the miss comes entirely from the list.

Self-study"Aware" is not yours to decide2 min▶

Whether a clock starts at the bad write, the first signal or the declaration is a contract or legal judgement. Session 1 made sure the record holds every candidate moment in UTC with its proof; tonight you add the sent and acknowledged times for each notice. With both, the person who owns the clock can answer without guessing. The leader's side of the same clocks is leader session 4.

Write Quarrybank's consumer notice

You are the responder for the bad load. The partition has been quarantined and the correction is under way. Compare two plans on the bench that differ only in who gets told, then write the notice and the notice list that become page 6 of the responder half of the plan.

Start on R3, then switch to R4. R3 has the containment playbook but tells only the complainers. The headline reads 7 + 3 with 4 never told. R4 adds one thing, lineage-driven notice, and the headline drops to 5 + 1 with 0 never told. Containment is 0h50 after the signal on both; the bench models plan times by stated rules, so the only change is who is on the list.

Compare R5 with T3. T3 is the trap: the same plan, but tell only who complained. Copy the four numbers into your record side by side.

Chase the acknowledgement, not the send. A notice in an unread inbox has changed nothing. Give every "no" in the acknowledged column an owner and a time to follow up, and write the follow-up in the record.

Check yourself

Three questions

Why does a notice list built from complaints miss the copies that matter most?

The partner extract and the forecast model showed nothing wrong to their users, so their owners never complained. Only the trace puts them on the list.

On the bench, R5 tells every consumer on the trace and T3 tells only who complained. What does T3 leave behind?

The response is otherwise the same plan. The four extra consumers and two extra decisions come entirely from who is on the notice list.

Who sets the GDPR 72-hour and PDPA notice deadlines for an incident involving personal data?

Legal and contract clocks are inputs from their owners. The responder's job is to make sure the record holds the aware candidates, the sent times and the acknowledgements.
